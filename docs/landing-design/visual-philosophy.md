# Visual philosophy: «Нэгжийн хэмжүүр» (Measured Unit)

A building is a stack of measured rooms, and each room is a number before it is a
home. This movement speaks only in that language: orthographic elevations, grids of
equal-height floors, cells whose widths are their areas. Form is never decorative;
every rectangle is a unit that could be bought, reserved or already owned. The
composition is a drawing sheet, held by margins and a title block, meticulously
crafted with the patience of a draughtsman who measured every line twice.

Space is orthogonal and quiet. Large fields of drafting paper surround one dense
object, so the eye goes straight to the mass of units and stays there. Rhythm comes
from repetition — floor after floor, cell after cell — interrupted only by the
irregular widths that the real areas produce. Scale is used deliberately: a single
monumental figure against tiny monospaced annotations, the contrast between the
building and its dimension lines carrying the hierarchy.

Colour is a state, not an ornament. Deep navy ink, a single teal for what is still
open, an ochre stripe for what is held, a recessive slate hatch for what is gone.
Texture replaces extra colour: stripes and hatches borrowed from section drawings,
each laid at a precise angle with painstaking consistency. Nothing glows, nothing
gradients into another hue; the restraint is the result of countless refinements.

Text is a whisper. A title block in monospace, a project name, a scale, a date —
the conventions of an architectural sheet, placed in the margins exactly where a
practitioner would expect them. Typography never explains; it labels. The final
artefact should look as though it was labored over by someone at the top of their
field: aligned to a single grid, no element touching another, every margin equal.
